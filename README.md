## Abstract summary

The thesis studies GNSS localization as a classification problem. A neural
network uses satellite positions and pseudorange measurements to predict which
geographic tile contains a receiver. The work covers a multi-input architecture,
preprocessing, and the handling of variability in GNSS measurements. It considers
the limitations of conventional positioning when interference, obstructions,
or multipath affect the signals, and identifies further model and data work
needed for practical use. The full abstract and thesis are available in the
university record above.
